# Method–object firewall: course content is discovered, not generated from the teaching controller

## Source jurisdictions and admissible uses

**METHOD**: educational psychology, reasoning controller, cognitive load, learning science, anti-slop, critical data visualization. Governs selection, pedagogy, explanation repair and evaluation. Never supplies a target-domain syllabus term, thinker, artwork, method, instrument or historical fact solely by appearing here.

**OBJECT**: disciplinary textbook (edition/page), authoritative documentation (version), primary text, empirical paper, certified syllabus, professional standard, official domain glossary. Supplies warranted course *content* and field terminology. Separate author claims from facts and contested interpretations.

**CURRICULUM**: explicit graduate profile, course-level learning outcomes, program competencies or occupational performance criteria. Supplies target performances, not everything that matters in a field. A single school's syllabus is an illustrative curriculum, not a universal norm.

**EXEMPLAR**: authenticated assignment, real artwork, code repository, professor's lecture exercise, official solution, artifact. Supplies practice examples; do not claim it is graded unless verified.

**CONTESTATION**: specialist critiques, serious professor blogs, critical blogs and community evidence. Supplies explicit rivals, failure cases and historically situated debates; do not treat a blog's stature or polemic as a primary empirical demonstration.

**LEARNER**: observed attempt, artifact, answer, test, approved goal; unique provenance. **GENERATED**: assistant's model, analogy, invented exercise or synthesis, labeled as such. METHOD must not be converted to OBJECT without independent subject evidence.

## Signifier-first topic discovery

The teaching language must be ordinary field vocabulary. Internal labels `signifier`, `controller`, `semantic descent`, `closure`, `source jurisdiction`, `warrant`, `operator`, `RMC` and `anti-slop` usually remain *behind the scenes*. Their appearance in a skill does not make them lesson subject matter. Do not coin shiny taxonomies or "experimental" technical terms as if the discipline already uses them. Emerging/experimental terminology may be taught when it is the genuine subject, linked to the originating author and explicitly called provisional or contested.

For each graduate-level performance or practical goal:
1. Translate it into a concrete observable task (e.g. diagnose a failed render, critique an argument, estimate a magnitude). Keep the approved profile text intact.
2. Collect actual module headings, repeated named concepts and prerequisites from at least two genuinely independent field sources where available: authoritative course syllabus, major field reference, field documentation, academic curriculum standard, or named practitioner's critical workflow. One source suffices for a proprietary/software-specific operator name, but version must be checked.
3. Generate a candidate **topic register** with canonical term in source language, accepted translation, exact source phrase and location, which kind of source, relation to graduate performance, observed disagreement, and confidence. Never claim corpus frequency or academic consensus based on a handful of pages.
4. Eliminate invalid synonyms, false friends, product-marketing labels, source-specific metaphors and rebranded pedagogical jargon. Preserve meaningful rival terms from recognized field traditions, label jurisdiction, no forced harmonization.
5. Form dependency graph from demonstrated prerequisites and material operations, not merely the alphabetical order or prominence of keywords.
6. Produce learning outcomes, evidence and labs for a small tractable slice; label gaps as *discovery debt*. Separate **student wording** from **internal planning terms**.

Question when uncertain: "Could a competent instructor in this domain recognize and locate this term in disciplinary material?" If not, either rename in the field's vocabulary or label as a locally coined metaphor and remove it from lesson headings.

## Method references from the learner's uploads, calibrated

- Wellington, *Secondary Education: The Key Concepts* (2006): organizing critical concepts, exposing competing interpretations and following the literature. Secondary-school context; not a universal standard for graduate education.
- Mahajan, *Street-Fighting Mathematics* (2010): within mathematics, exemplifies recognized techniques such as dimensional analysis, easy cases, lumping and pictorial proofs; as teaching method inspiration, encourages witnessed problems before abstract recital. It is ALSO an OBJECT source when the subject really is applied mathematical problem-solving. Uploaded edition indicates CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 US; follow actual license.
- Lawson, *How Designers Think*, 4th ed. (2005): discussion of design as repeated reframing/solution conjecture; design research, not a guaranteed stage sequence, especially when transferred to other fields.
- Dewey, *How We Think*: a philosophical/pragmatic account of reflective inquiry. The edition and relevant section must be located before making precise attribution.
- Paul & Elder, *Thinker's Guide to Scientific Thinking*: possible method critique for question/assumption/evidence, not an automatic scientific subject reference. Verify the edition/section before citing.
- Piechurska-Kuciel, *The Big Five in SLA* (2020): personality and second-language acquisition within one domain, NOT permission to infer a learner's immutable style/personality from a chat.
- Kahneman, *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (2011): possible bias/decision-method reference; do not turn named heuristics into deterministic learner diagnoses.
- Van der Ryn, *Design for an Empathic World* (2013): design/participation context; do not treat as proof of a universally effective teaching intervention.
- Ivantsiv, *Polyglot Notes* (2020): author-oriented practical language study; individual recipes and time splits are not general cross-domain evidence.

Other uploaded file formats may be consulted after inspecting their actual text. A filename is not a license, a validated claim or proof of reading.
