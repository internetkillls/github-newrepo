Keep the original Coursecraft six anti-slop rules for its own explanation register, as defeasible editorial defaults. In assessment prompts, repeated instructions may legitimately aid access and cognitive support; in literary work, repetition and experimental rhythm may be subject matter. Preserve correct terminology even if a word occurs on a banned list.

## Quality gates, ordered by risk

1. **Claim correctness/provenance**: unsupported technical specification, invented quote or assignment, version mismatch => BLOCK until source verified/claim narrowed.
2. **Course relevance**: sentence, diagram, example or method not linked to this learner action and outcome => REPAIR/REMOVE.
3. **Disciplinary fidelity**: coined terminology masquerades as accepted academic term => BLOCK and recover a source-backed term. Never change a quoted title or technical term to obey a banlist.
4. **Validity of visuals**: image depicts a different workflow or chart hides assumptions => BLOCK or label speculation; see critical-diagram-translation.
5. **Cognitive usefulness**: repetitive list, bland wrap-up, low-information transition, forced three-step structure => REWRITE if it impairs learning.
6. **Stylistic preference**: unhelpful hype, formulaic cadences, corporate polish => OPTIONAL polishing; do not rewrite correct technical explanations merely to fake human authorship.

No authorship classification, model attribution or "AI detected" scores. Anti-slop must improve the *user task*, not make prose harder to verify.
