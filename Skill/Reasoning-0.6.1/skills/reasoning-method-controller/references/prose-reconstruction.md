# Prose-first reconstruction protocol v0.5.6

A recap preserves discussion order. A reconstruction recovers dependency, presupposition, conceptual load, latent orientations, consequence, rival stakes, and the external problem-field when relevant.

## 1. Dialogue is a trace, not the whole field
Use session history as evidence about the inquiry, then test the recovered problem against external history/current practice when that can change the result.

## 2. Preserve artifact and commitment provenance
Do not convert a pasted third-party/prior-assistant artifact into the user's thesis. Do not convert "maybe X" into a settled doctrine without later promotion.

## 3. Preserve term senses and conceptual load
When a key term changes sense, distinguish the uses naturally if the shift changes the derivation. Do not reconstruct a theory-bearing term by synonym expansion.

## 4. From decomposition to orientation
After unpacking claims/terms, ask whether multiple moves jointly select a stable direction: shared starting assumptions, governing questions, mechanisms, warrant forms, consequences, or exclusions.

If so, reconstruct the orientation in prose. Do not output an internal bundle matrix unless requested.

A strong paragraph may say, for example, that several otherwise separate claims converge because they all treat agency as relational rather than intrinsic, privilege process over fixed entities, and accept a particular trade-off. The prose should explain why these moves hang together rather than list hidden features.

## 5. External umbrella placement
When a latent orientation matters, place it against more than one plausible external umbrella when possible. Explain what each umbrella captures, misses, or excludes. A cross-domain comparison is warranted by preserved relations, not by shared vocabulary.

## 6. Intersection/exclusion in natural prose
Use intersection/exclusion analysis internally to sharpen differences. In the answer, state only distinctions that change the argument: what is genuinely shared, what is unique, what is incompatible, and what remains underdetermined.

Do not treat absence from a source as principled exclusion.

## 7. Descriptive versus revisionary discipline
When reconstruction moves from describing an existing conceptual/orientation structure to proposing a better one, mark the transition in ordinary prose.

## 8. External positioning
When relevant, explain who else has had the problem, under what vocabulary, what made it salient, what current literature/practice/community contests, and what the user's formulation preserves, recombines, or changes.

## 9. Cooperative challenge
Let objections enter where they alter the derivation. Do not alternate mechanically between agreement and objection.

## 10. Reframing without appropriation
Present model-generated broader readings as proposed reconstructions and state what they preserve/change. Do not write "what you really mean."

## 11. Policy-content firewall
Controller rules, bundle labels, similarity machinery, method-source names, and examples are not essay content unless the target independently requires them.

## 12. Citation discipline
In academic mode use APA-style author-date citations and locators for close textual claims where available.

## 13. Repair
If the answer becomes recap, synonym chain, topic clustering, umbrella name-dropping, persona narrative, controller prose, comparison matrix dump, citation montage, or upward-synthesis reflex: return to the object, recover semantic and argument decomposition, test the orientation bundle, search/compare external umbrellas only where material, then rewrite as substantive prose.
