# Target venues and the separate ladders of publication, exposure, revenue and career

## Venue is not publisher
Springer Nature, Oxford University Press, De Gruyter Brill and MIT Press are publishers/imprints. The actionable unit is a SPECIFIC journal, its editorial scope, accepted article type, its current authors instructions and direct submission portal. Do not claim an article can be submitted to a publisher brand without a venue. Distinguish peer-reviewed article vs review, commentary, essay, book chapter, book proposal, preprint, proceedings. Verify discipline and article type before suggesting a target.

Research checklist per candidate: official journal name and exact URL; fits 2–3 recently published articles?; journal subject scope and methodology; typical references and manuscript structure; accepted format; word count and anonymized review rules; peer review; open-access/hybrid; APC/waiver/currency (never guess); licensing; preprint and prior-publication policy; AI and author contribution declarations; conflicts and ethical review when relevant; submission URL; last checked date. Use Think.Check.Submit to scrutinize transparency/predation, not just brand reputation.

## Scholarly transformation examples (hypotheses, not user-ready outputs)
- News: computational models challenge a philosophical theory → literature-gap memo → reasoned critical argument with proper novelty search → journal article or venue-accepted commentary.
- News: generative visual systems → reproducible TouchDesigner demonstration plus short methods documentation → open repository or portfolio → research article only if there is a genuine independently evidenced contribution and journal fit.
- A Zettelkasten/Leaving Note is an inquiry trace; it is NOT itself a research result or publishable paper until transformed with argument, sources, authorship, format and research integrity.

## Professional and creator media
- **LinkedIn:** evidence-based professional essay, project case study, critical review, short commentary, career progress or demo; target readers and professional value. Validate post/article/newsletter options and analytics from current LinkedIn Help. Don't falsely promise distribution reach.
- **Instagram:** visually demonstrable carousel, short motion study, process breakdown, portfolio reel. Respect platform rights, image/music licensing, accessibility, privacy and disclosure rules. A scholarly claim needs source links that readers can consult.
- **TikTok:** explain an idea through a genuine one-minute visual/audio demonstration, a researched explainer with caveats, or a process/research diary. Time-box development and avoid algorithm-chasing; check current business/monetization eligibility by country and program using official rules.
- **GitHub:** own project with README/license/test and reproducible project, community documentation/test issue, bugfix PR or reviewed contribution. Distinguish repository publishing, contribution attempt and accepted patch.

## Four non-interchangeable outcomes
1. `PUBLICATION`: verifiable live URL, DOI, repo tag or editor receipt — identify subtype and peer-review status.
2. `EXPOSURE`: who encountered it, how (impressions, plays, referential links), reach vs engagement, audience match, permissions; report as observational platform data.
3. `CAREER`: demonstrated skill and a concrete next action from a relevant person (interview invitation, portfolio request, collaboration, citation, speaker invite, actual application). Keep confidential contacts private.
4. `MONETIZATION`: distinguish receipts from speculation. Mechanisms include commissioned explainers / sound-design / motion graphics services, consultancy, sponsorship, licensing, teaching/workshop, paid research or editorial work, legitimate platform creator programs and sale of owned assets. For each specify PAYER, PURCHASED DELIVERABLE, QUALIFICATION, PAYMENT TRIGGER, COSTS, PLATFORM CUT, COUNTRY / tax / licensing if relevant, transaction evidence. 'More followers' is not a business model; `views × invented RPM` is not revenue evidence.

## Responsible distribution experiment (optional)
One original core artifact may be translated to: scholarly research output (if genuine novel research), LinkedIn explanatory article, Instagram process carousel, TikTok short demonstration, GitHub reproducible proof. Provenance and citations remain attached; each adaptation is distinct and independently checked, NOT auto-published. Start by selecting one public channel only. Define one falsifiable audience question and review after a small reasonable observation period; do not impose a content treadmill.
