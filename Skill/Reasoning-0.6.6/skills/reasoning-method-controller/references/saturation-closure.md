# Saturation and closure: dual criterion

## 1. Why candidate stability is insufficient

A model can become stable because the search space was narrow, one vocabulary dominated retrieval, or the candidate itself determined what counted as relevant. Therefore "new sources no longer change my answer" is not sufficient closure.

## 2. Closure requires two independent checks

### A. Candidate stability
The current answer/decision survives:
- at least one serious rival or negative case appropriate to the inquiry;
- consequential version/date differences where relevant;
- additional evidence from a nonredundant source position.

### B. Evidence-topology adequacy
The inquiry has covered the structurally distinct evidence positions needed for its claim.

Possible positions include, only when relevant:
- direct/primary or authoritative evidence;
- a serious rival, dissent, or negative case;
- historical/earlier formulation when genealogy matters;
- current/final version when recency matters;
- runtime/practice evidence when implementation claims are made;
- community/discourse signals when the claim concerns live discourse;
- adjacent-field evidence when terminology or ownership of the problem is contested.

No universal quota or mandatory source category applies. The topology follows the claim and inquiry regime.

## 3. Anti-circularity rule

Define required evidence positions from the question, claim type, field structure, and known rival possibilities—not solely from the preferred candidate model.

## 4. Stop conditions

Stop when both conditions hold and the next nonredundant evidence position is unlikely to change the decision at the requested scope.

If candidate stability holds but topology adequacy does not: continue or narrow the claim.
If topology adequacy holds but candidates remain unstable: continue discriminating tests.
If tools/access prevent completion: state a research debt; do not claim exhaustive coverage, consensus, novelty, or absence.
