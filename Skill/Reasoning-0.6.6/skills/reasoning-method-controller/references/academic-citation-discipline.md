# Academic citation discipline — APA author-date

Use this protocol when the destination genre is academic/scholarly writing and the user has not requested another citation system.

## 1. Body citations are mandatory for sourced claims

Use APA-style author-date citations in the prose:
- parenthetical: `(Author, Year)`;
- narrative: `Author (Year)`;
- two authors: `(Author & Author, Year)`; narrative connective follows the language of the sentence while preserving author identities;
- three or more authors: `(Author et al., Year)` / `Author et al. (Year)` according to APA 7 conventions.

A bibliography without body citations is insufficient.

## 2. Exact quotations and close textual claims

Direct quotations require a locator:
- `(Author, Year, p. 12)`;
- `(Author, Year, pp. 12–13)`;
- paragraph, section, chapter, or other stable locator when pages are unavailable.

For close philosophical/textual reconstruction, add page/chapter/section locators whenever available and useful even when paraphrasing, especially when the interpretation depends on a specific locus.

## 3. Versions and chapters

Cite the actual edition/version consulted when version matters. Do not merge original and revised editions silently.

For an authored chapter in an edited collection, attribute the chapter's substantive claim to the chapter author, not merely the editor of the volume.

## 4. Primary versus secondary citation

Prefer the primary source for authorial claims when available. If only a secondary source was actually consulted, do not write as though the primary was inspected. Mark secondary citation according to APA conventions or state the evidential limitation in prose.

## 5. Reference list

For substantial academic outputs, include a consistent reference list unless the user explicitly asks not to. Preserve author names, year, title, source/container, volume/issue/pages where applicable, and DOI/URL where appropriate.

## 6. Platform-source markers

Platform citation chips, hidden retrieval markers, raw URLs, or footnote numbers do not replace APA author-date citations in the academic prose. They may coexist when the host platform requires them, but the human-readable academic artifact must remain self-sufficient.

## 7. Do not overcite

Citations should support claims with external provenance. Common connective reasoning and clearly marked model synthesis do not need a citation on every sentence. When one paragraph contains multiple sourced moves, place citations where their scope is unambiguous.
