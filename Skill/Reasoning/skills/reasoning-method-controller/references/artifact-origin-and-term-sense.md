# Artifact origin and term-sense provenance

## 1. Artifact origin

When supplied material matters to attribution, distinguish its origin as evidence permits:

- `USER-AUTHORED` — explicitly authored by the user.
- `USER-QUOTED` — user quotes another source/person.
- `THIRD-PARTY` — externally authored artifact supplied by the user.
- `PRIOR-ASSISTANT` — generated by this or another assistant in earlier interaction.
- `GENERATED-PASTE` — system-generated or exported paste whose authorship may differ from the user.
- `SOURCE-EXTRACT` — excerpt from a document/source.
- `UNKNOWN-ORIGIN` — origin cannot be established.

Rules:
- supplied-by-user ≠ authored-by-user;
- authored-by-user ≠ endorsed-now;
- quoted-by-user ≠ agreed-with;
- prior-assistant output ≠ user position;
- critique target ≠ commitment.

Track endorsement/stance separately from origin.

## 2. Pasted-artifact gate

Before treating pasted material as a premise about the user's view, ask:
- Who authored it?
- Why was it supplied: endorsement, critique, comparison, editing, extraction, continuation, or unknown?
- Did the user explicitly adopt any claim from it?

If not established, cite or describe it as an artifact, not as the user's belief.

## 3. Term-sense registry

Use only for terms that bear an inference.

Internal fields:

```text
TERM
SENSE-ID
USE-ORIGIN: user | source | field | model | local-stipulation
DEFINITION / EXEMPLAR
SCOPE
INFERENCES LICENSED
SHIFT-FROM
SHIFT-REASON
AFFECTED CLAIMS
```

Do not expose this registry by default.

## 4. Sense-shift gate

A shift requires repair when it changes:
- what counts as an instance;
- the relevant literature/field;
- a premise or conclusion;
- formalization or measurement;
- comparison with a rival;
- what the user is taken to have committed to.

If a shift is harmless shorthand, do not overburden the discussion.

## 5. Ownership is provenance, not property

"Owner" of a term-use means whose usage is being tracked, not who legally or metaphysically owns the word. A single term may legitimately have multiple concurrent senses; the problem is silent inheritance across senses.
