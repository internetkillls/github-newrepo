# Thematic, linguistic, and epistemic-jurisdiction protocol

## 1. Do not start with `CLAIM`

The input unit can be a proposition, question, command, definition, quotation, report, conjecture, stipulation, fragment, example, or metalinguistic expression. Classify only when the distinction can change an inference.

## 2. Epistemic-Linguistic Jurisdiction card

```text

CONTENT:

```

## 3. Thematic articulation

A thematic cluster is not merely lexical similarity. Test:
- material relevance to the current theme;
- functional role in the inquiry;
- family of uses;
- jurisdiction and warrant;
- whether the item is theme, field, or margin.

Never let thematic coding overwrite quotation/report status, historical provenance, or the difference between source assertion and MODEL reconstruction.

## 4. Typical jurisdiction distinctions

### Historical
Truth bearer: event/chronology/context claim.
Typical warrant: archival/primary historical evidence, corroboration, contextual chronology.
Not automatically licensed: hermeneutic meaning or causal mechanism.

### Philological/textual
Truth bearer: wording, edition, manuscript, translation, textual relation.
Typical warrant: verified locus, edition/manuscript comparison.
Not automatically licensed: author's systematic position.

### Bibliographic/library
Truth bearer: identity, version, publication/record relation.
Typical warrant: canonical record, identifier, publisher/repository metadata.

### Scientometric
Truth bearer: relations among publications/authors/citations/fields.
Typical warrant: defined database, coverage rules, citation/co-occurrence analysis.
Not automatically licensed: substantive truth of cited theories.

### Empirical/sectoral science
Truth bearer: observational/experimental/model result within stated population/context.
Typical warrant: data, method, uncertainty, replication/triangulation as appropriate.

### Formal proof
Truth bearer: theorem relative to axioms/rules/semantics.
Typical warrant: proof under mapped formal conditions.
Not automatically licensed: empirical truth in a target domain.

### Thought experiment / modal reasoning
Truth bearer: possibility/necessity/counterexample under stipulated conditions.
Typical warrant: coherent stipulation + modal/inferential bridge.

### Phenomenological
Truth bearer: disciplined description of structures of appearance/experience under the chosen method.
Typical warrant: phenomenological analysis, not empirical frequency by default.

### Hermeneutic
Truth bearer: interpretation of meaning/function within text/tradition/context.
Typical warrant: textual coherence, context, explanatory reach, rival readings.
Not automatically licensed: historical event or empirical mechanism.

### Normative
Truth bearer: reason/obligation/value evaluation.
Typical warrant: normative premises, consistency, reflective/dialectical support.

## 5. Linguistic dependencies

Check context when indexicals, tense, anaphora, presupposition, quotation, reported speech, or attitude ascriptions materially alter content. When discourse dependence matters, represent state transitions `D0 → utterance → D1` rather than isolated propositions.

## 6. Failure signatures

- Same theme, different jurisdictions treated as mutually evidential → JURISDICTION FLATTENING.
- Quotation treated as endorsement → FORCE/MENTION COLLAPSE.
- User's term given one essence despite diverse uses → USE-CLUSTER FAILURE.
- Lexically recurring word becomes organizing theme without material relevance → THEMATIC SALIENCE ERROR.
- Hermeneutic reading presented as historical fact → WARRANT TRANSPORT WITHOUT LICENSE.
